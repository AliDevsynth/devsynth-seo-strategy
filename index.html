SEO (search engine optimization) means making it easy for Google to find our pages, understand them, trust them, and show them to the right people. This guide explains how it works, the main techniques, and what each one means for Devsynth. About 25 minutes to read. Words with a dotted underline are also in the "How to read this" tab.

The short version

  1. Google has to find and read the page (technical SEO).
  2. The page has to answer exactly what the searcher wants (keywords, intent, content).
  3. Other trusted sites have to vouch for us (links, reviews, mentions).
  4. It takes time: 3 to 6 months for a new site to move, 6 to 12 months for competitive phrases. Brand searches ("devsynth") move in days to weeks.

4. Keywords: choosing what to rank for

  • Search volume: how many searches a month. More is better, but only if the people searching could become clients.
  • Difficulty: how strong the pages already ranking are. A new site with few links should pick low-difficulty phrases first.
  • CPC (cost per click): what advertisers pay. High CPC usually means buyers search it ("software modernization services" about USD 51 a click), so it's worth ranking for.
  • Head vs long-tail: "mobile app development" (huge volume, nearly impossible) vs "cross platform mobile app development services" (2,400 a month, low difficulty). Long-tail phrases are easier and the searchers are closer to buying.
  • One page per phrase group: each phrase should have one clear page. If two of our pages target the same phrase, they compete with each other (called cannibalization) and both rank worse.

How we did it: Seobility gave volume, difficulty and the pages ranking today for each service; each service page now targets one main phrase plus 3 to 6 related ones (research/content/keyword-map-r3.json).

6. Content: what makes Google (and buyers) trust a page

  • Helpful first: write for the buyer, not for Google. Answer the real question, plainly, early.
  • Show experience: real projects, real numbers, real screenshots. This is our advantage: most agency blogs are generic. Our posts mention EVDC, LifeLinq, Meelz with facts.
  • Topic clusters: one main page on a topic (the "pillar", e.g. the AI service page) plus several posts on sub-questions (RAG vs fine-tuning, AI agents for business), all linked together. Google sees we cover the topic in depth.
  • Freshness: update posts when facts change; Google prefers current content for fast-moving topics like AI.
  • Cadence: 2 good posts a month beats 10 thin ones.
  • AI-written content: allowed by Google if it's helpful and accurate. Mass-produced, unchecked AI text is penalized. We use AI to draft and humans to check facts.
  • Never: keyword stuffing (repeating the phrase unnaturally), copied content, pages made only for search engines.

8. Links and authority: the part that takes longest

A link from another site to ours is a vote of trust. Quality beats quantity: one link from a respected tech publication is worth more than 1,000 from junk sites. Links from unrelated spam sites (we have 19) count for nothing and are ignored.

9. Brand and entity: owning the "devsynth" search

Google tries to understand companies as "entities": one company, one name, one set of facts, linked profiles. When signals conflict (DevSynth vs Devsynth vs Devsynth Innovations, different addresses), Google gets confused and mixes in other results.

  • One name everywhere: "Devsynth".
  • Same facts everywhere: description, services, countries, founding date (that's what the Profile kit is for).
  • Connect the profiles: sameAs schema on our site, and every profile links back to devsynth.us.
  • Reviews: a buyer googling "devsynth reviews" should find our Clutch reviews, not another company's complaints.
  • Clean up old results: delete or rename at the source, then use Google's removal tool.

10. Local SEO: maps and "near me"

For searches with a place ("software company Toronto"), Google shows a map with 3 businesses (the map pack). To appear you need a Google Business Profile with a real, staffed address, consistent name/address/phone across the web, reviews, and photos. Virtual offices get suspended. For Devsynth this is optional: most of our buyers search without a city, and our US profile already works as a service-area business.

11. AI search: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews

More buyers now ask AI assistants "who can build our MVP?". These tools build answers from websites, directories, reviews and articles. Getting mentioned (cited) needs the same basics plus:

  • Direct answers: short, quotable sentences (our FAQ style).
  • Consistent facts across many sites: AI repeats what several sources agree on.
  • Being on the lists AI reads: Clutch, G2, "top companies" articles, Reddit discussions.
  • Clear structure: headings, schema, plain definitions.

Test it monthly: ask ChatGPT and Perplexity "custom software development company for startups in the USA" and "what is Devsynth?", and note what they say.

14. Common mistakes and myths

  • Myth: "SEO is a one-time setup." It's ongoing: content, links and fixes every month.
  • Myth: "More keywords on the page = higher rank." Stuffing hurts. Write naturally.
  • Myth: "Meta keywords tag matters." Google ignores it.
  • Mistake: changing URLs without 301 redirects (loses all built-up trust).
  • Mistake: leaving noindex on after launch.
  • Mistake: buying cheap backlinks or "SEO packages" from cold emails.
  • Mistake: many thin pages on the same topic instead of one strong one.
  • Mistake: judging results after 2 weeks. Give non-brand SEO 3 to 6 months.
  • Mistake: different company names and facts on different profiles.

  • Visib. (visibility) is one number for the whole keyword list: high positions on high-volume keywords count more. Use the trend, not the size of the number.
  • URL shows which of our pages ranks. If it is the wrong page (a blog post for a service phrase), the service page needs work.
  • S.V. search volume a month (USA), CPC what advertisers pay a click, Comp. how hard the competition is, Intent I = informational, C = commercial, N = navigational.
  • The two screen icons at the top right switch between desktop and mobile rankings; they differ. The date box and Days pick the comparison period.
  • Click a keyword's name to open its panel: Rankings (history), Landing pages, Competitors (their positions for that keyword) and AI Overview (whether Google's AI answer cites us).

  • Keyword Research, URL/Domain mode (1 check): the phrases a competitor ranks for in Google's top 20, with volume. Worked for 8 of 8 domains we tried, in google.com and google.ca.
  • TF*IDF: compares a page's words with the top 10 results; good for writing service pages and posts.
  • Ranking Checker and SERP checks for single phrases.

What it can't do (Ahrefs and Semrush can)

  • Keyword gap needs us to rank. The Competitor analysis (Gap) mode returned "No data available" for devsynth.us vs tkxel.com and arbisoft.com because we have no rankings yet. It will work later.
  • Only Google's top 20 per competitor, capped at about 250 rows, and the index is small: mvpdevelopment.company is #1 in live Google for "mvp development company", but Seobility lists only 10 keywords for it and not that one.
  • No top pages report (which competitor pages bring the most traffic).
  • Backlink Checker shows the strongest links, not a full exportable list, on Premium. No anchor text report, no backlink monitoring.
  • No competition percentage for google.ca rows, and no traffic estimates per page.
